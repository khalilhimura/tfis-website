Reviewer guide / Mind → Memory → System

Executive summary

Accumulated Curiosity — executive summary, architectural blueprint, and field kit guide.

The proposition: an individual can build greater independent capability by turning questions into tested judgment, preserving that judgment in sovereign memory, and using it to direct bounded AI workflows that deliver useful work.

Read the full book · Explore the chapter artifacts · Inspect the SSA-CMM ladder

The core thesis

AI makes answers and drafts easier to obtain. The scarce work moves toward choosing a worthwhile question, evaluating evidence, retaining useful corrections, and deciding which responsibilities a system has earned. Accumulated Curiosity develops those abilities through one project carried across 24 chapters. Its unit of progress is an inspectable piece of work: an attempt, a judgment, a repair, and a record that improves the next attempt.

The cognitive premise is that curiosity compounds when its results remain usable. A question becomes valuable through investigation and application; an experience becomes memory through selection, review, and later use. The reader learns to explain a decision without outsourcing its meaning, while retrieving the details and evidence needed to make that decision well. Polymathic thinking contributes testable connections across domains, with the limits of each analogy made explicit.

The architectural premise is that durable knowledge needs governance. Sovereign memory preserves the distinction between a source, an inference, a decision, and an instruction. It identifies who may change a record, how a current version is selected, what should be retained or removed, and how the work can be reconstructed elsewhere. SovMem supplies part of the book’s TFIS design context; the practical methods can begin with ordinary documents and folders. A Cognitive OS is the operating procedure that connects this memory to daily work, review, interruption, and resumption.

The execution premise is that delegation requires a demonstrated task and an explicit contract. An agent needs authorized inputs, a defined return, limited permissions, repair limits, and escalation conditions. A person needs evidence with which to judge the returned work. Where multiple roles cooperate, their handoffs must refer to the same source and candidate version. An approved correction must reach the maintained memory and influence an appropriate later attempt before the learning loop can be called closed.

The economic premise is conditional: greater delivery capability can support a solo enterprise when it meets a buyer’s need at sustainable cost and effort. The book connects capability to an offer, a product experiment, delivery, support, unit economics, and capacity. Revenue, owner income, profit, and company valuation remain distinct. The million-dollar destination is an ambition to examine through assumptions and constraints; it is not an outcome promised by AI adoption or a maturity label.

The intended reader is a curious professional or aspiring solo founder willing to attempt, inspect, and revise real work. Services, education, and software provide application paths. Coding is optional for the core exercises. The book’s defining structure is the continuity of its artifacts: the output of an early inquiry becomes evidence, authority, or a constraint for later execution.

Review lens: evaluate whether that continuity holds. Can a reader trace a consequential claim to its source, a workflow to its current instruction, a review to the actual candidate, and a commercial decision to its assumptions? The opening guide explains the progression; Chapter 23 defines the scope of capability claims.

The system at a glance

Mind → Memory → System → new evidence → the next inquiry. The arcs describe a learning sequence. In operation, delivery and review send corrections back into memory and questions back into inquiry.

Arc Inputs Process Operational outputs
01 · Mind — Chapters 1–7 One bounded task, a starting attempt, sources, and unresolved questions. Establish a baseline; check claims; select an inquiry; explain assumptions; test a connection; apply and review a correction. A claim–evidence ledger, inquiry brief, and learning loop that preserve what changed and why.
02 · Memory — Chapters 8–15 The project’s sources, attempts, judgments, and current instructions. Inventory; govern; separate memory roles; distil with review; connect records; verify saved judgment; rehearse recovery. A memory charter, maintained route to current instructions, verdict ledger, operating manual, and portability record.
03 · System — Chapters 16–24 A recoverable working set, demonstrated task, review criteria, and a prospective buyer problem. Demonstrate capability; bound delegation; inspect handoffs; operate delivery; test an offer; model effort and economics. Delegation and system contracts, enterprise runbook, product experiment, unit-economics model, capability portfolio, and a ninety-day plan.

The handoffs that carry the architecture

  1. Claim–evidence ledger → inquiry and review. The ledger separates supported statements from unresolved assumptions. Those distinctions travel into the questions asked and the criteria used to assess later outputs.
  2. Learning loop → governed memory. A failed attempt and its correction become material to retain, classify, and review. A polished summary alone does not establish which instruction is current.
  3. Memory charter + current instruction → delegation contract. The charter constrains retention and authority; the instruction supplies the task rule. The contract specifies what an executor may do with the authorized packet.
  4. Candidate + source + review policy → scoped verdict. The reviewer judges the particular candidate against its evidence. A verdict on an earlier version cannot approve a revision it never examined.
  5. Approved verdict → verified save → later use. Reopen the saved record, supply it through the current route, and examine a changed-input attempt. Retrieval and correct application are separate observations.
  6. Reviewed delivery → commercial experiment → economics. A useful internal output becomes an offer to test. Buyer response, acceptance, support effort, and costs inform whether to repeat, narrow, reprice, or stop the work.

These are a synthesis of the book’s operating dependencies, rather than a claim that every chapter runs as one automatic pipeline. The Practice Index identifies the primary artifact carried forward from each chapter.

One correction, end to end

In the fictional Himura Inc. case, a draft invents a date from the word “soon.” The review preserves the missing date and the reason for the correction. The approved instruction must remain reachable through the maintained current-version route. A later input that explicitly supplies a date tests whether the correction was understood: changing every date to “unknown” would fail by overgeneralizing it.

The architectural test is therefore evidence → scoped judgment → current instruction → changed behavior. Inspect the distillation record and verdict ledger for the worked records. These are teaching specimens; a reader’s operational claim needs their own saved result and later run.

The six-level SSA-CMM ladder

SSA-CMM means Solo Systems Architect Capability Maturity Model. The book uses the six canonical TFIS homepage labels below. The evidence column summarizes the book’s practical interpretation; it is not a certification rubric. Capability is specific to a domain and operating conditions, and the three book arcs do not assign a reader a level merely through completion.

Level Capability Evidence to inspect
L0 Task Operator Original input, direct attempt, review criteria, corrections, and effort: a baseline for comparable work.
L1 AI-Assisted Operator Actual assisted attempts with first outputs, retained errors, and the human help required to reach the completion standard.
L2 Workflow Orchestrator A complete sequence with inspectable inputs and returns; a rehearsal showing what happens when a prerequisite is absent.
L3 Agent Supervisor Execution within a bounded contract, meaningful review, an appropriate escalation, and evidence that a mistaken judgment can be repaired.
L4 Multi-Agent Architect Coordinated roles using matching source and candidate identities, plus a retained judgment verified in a relevant later attempt. An agent count is insufficient.
L5 Sovereign Architect A selected operation reconstructed after a meaningful dependency change, with current authority, permissions, behavior, recovery, and remaining dependencies examined.

The TFIS Functional Life source uses alternative labels at L0, L1, L2, and L4, including “Loop Engineer” at L4. Chapter 23’s source note discloses those differences. The model is a proposed progression; the book does not present it as an independently validated professional standard or a predictor of business success.

Chapter artifact guide

Use this as the expanded structural synopsis. Each entry identifies a prerequisite, an operation, and a primary output. Chapter links open the full argument and field assignment; artifact links open the reusable working document. Keep failed candidates and superseded judgments alongside the corrected record so later decisions remain traceable.

Mind · establish a question worth pursuing

01 · The Future Is Solo
Bring one recurring task. Record direct work, define acceptance criteria, and compare five saved-prompt attempts, including corrections. Output: Capability baseline — the starting evidence for judging useful assistance; five attempts are a practice exercise, not a reliability guarantee.

02 · The End of Knowledge Scarcity
Bring the first output and its consequential claims. Inspect source support, distinguish observation from assumption, and narrow an overreaching statement. Output: Claim–evidence ledger — traceable support, limitations, status, and next actions.

03 · Curiosity Is a Capability
Bring the unfinished work and unresolved claims. Select one question that can change a decision, bound the inquiry, and retain its result. Output: Curiosity map — a chosen inquiry with reasons and deferred alternatives.

04 · The Question Is the Unit of Learning
Bring the selected uncertainty. State your expectation, evidence route, requested output, and what would change your mind; attempt and review it. Output: Inquiry brief — a question and evaluation method that travel beyond the chat.

05 · First Principles, Axioms, and Mental Models
Bring a consequential concept or process. Explain how it should work, separate constraints from assumptions, and test one weak premise. Output: Concept deconstruction — an inspectable mechanism and an evidence-backed revision.

06 · The Polymath Advantage
Bring a project difficulty and an idea from another domain. Map the relationship, identify a mismatch, and compare the adaptation with the original approach. Output: Connection map — a tested analogy, including where it breaks.

07 · The Accumulated Curiosity Loop
Bring the inquiry and relevant earlier records. Connect explanation, test, application, and review; run a second pass on changed input. Output: Learning loop — a trace from the first correction into the next attempt.

Memory · make useful judgment available again

08 · The Second Brain Is Not Enough
Bring the project’s existing records. Recover a consequential correction from a fresh starting point and distinguish finding it from applying it correctly. Output: Memory inventory — dependencies, locations, retrieval gaps, and current-state clarity.

09 · The Sovereign Memory Principle
Bring that inventory. Define retention, access, authority, correction, deletion, and portability; test the rules on a small working set. Output: Memory charter — explicit governance for what later workflows may retain and change.

10 · The Architecture of Remembering
Bring governed records and a task to resume. Separate immediate context, project state, reviewed reusable knowledge, and recovery copies; restore a selected package. Output: Memory lanes — actual locations and separate observations about files, links, versions, and behavior.

11 · The Knowledge Distillation Pipeline
Bring a useful exchange, source material, and an existing instruction. Distil claims, decisions, reasons, and limits; review the change and verify its current destination. Output: Distillation record — a retained correction whose authority and provenance survive compression.

12 · The Personal Knowledge Graph
Bring claims, instructions, attempts, and questions. Connect them with named relationships and inspect what a change affects. Output: Knowledge map — navigable evidence and dependency relationships that can be checked after a move.

13 · The Memory Flywheel
Bring a reviewed attempt and a correction worth preserving. Record scope and reason, verify the approved save, and inspect later use on changed input. Output: Verdict ledger — a judgment linked to evidence, correction, and its applicability.

14 · The Cognitive OS
Bring the current instructions, evidence routes, and verdicts. Define how work starts, pauses, resumes, is reviewed, and finishes. Output: Operating manual — a usable route into the task and its unresolved conditions.

15 · The Sovereignty Test
Bring a defined working set and its permissions. Move it, reconstruct context, and test current instructions and useful behavior separately. Output: Portability drill — observed continuity and remaining dependencies; readable files alone do not prove provider independence.

System · demonstrate, deliver, and inspect the economics

16 · From Knowledge to Capability
Bring a recoverable task packet and review criteria. Attempt fresh input, retain the first candidate, correct it, and account for assistance and effort. Output: Capability demonstration — evidence for the next bounded responsibility.

17 · The Agentic Workforce
Bring that demonstrated task, authorized sources, and current instruction. Specify allowed steps, returned objects, repair limits, permissions, and stops. Output: Delegation contract — a responsibility an executor can carry out and a person can inspect.

18 · The Human-in-the-Loop Principle
Bring a candidate, its sources, and the delegation boundary. Place human judgment at consequential decisions and rehearse withdrawing a mistaken acceptance. Output: Review policy — evidence requirements, scoped verdicts, and correction procedures matched to review capacity.

19 · The Solo Systems Architect
Bring distinct roles and their input/output contracts. Match job, source, candidate, and comparison identities; expose and repair a broken handoff. Output: System contracts — explicit interfaces that prevent stale or mismatched evidence from authorizing work.

20 · The Architecture of a Solo Enterprise
Bring a tested internal workflow. Place it within intake, scope, preparation, review, delivery, support, capacity, and recovery. Output: Enterprise runbook — an operating procedure with business assumptions kept visible.

21 · From Curiosity to Product
Bring a demonstrated capability and an identifiable buyer problem. Make a bounded offer, record the response, and evaluate delivery against agreed criteria. Output: Product experiment — separate evidence for interest, purchase, acceptance, and still-unobserved outcomes.

22 · From Product to Value
Bring offer terms, delivery effort, and explicit cost assumptions. Model revenue, cash costs, owner time, fixed costs, and capacity; change a consequential assumption. Output: Unit economics — a decision model for repeating, repricing, narrowing, or stopping an offer.

23 · The SSA-CMM Ladder
Bring actual demonstrations from one recurring domain. Tie each capability claim to its conditions and evidence; repair an overstatement. Output: Capability portfolio — defensible claims and the next missing demonstration.

24 · The Million-Dollar Solo Company
Bring the portfolio, product evidence, economics, and personal constraints. Translate the ambition into a ninety-day sequence of evidence-producing decisions. Output: Solo-enterprise plan — a bounded operating and commercial plan with room to change direction.

How to review the book

Review objective Start here Question to resolve
Technical rigor Chapters 11, 13, 17–19, and 23. Do provenance, authority, review, persistence, and handoffs remain inspectable? Which demonstrations are still required?
Commercial premise Chapters 20–22 and 24. Is the offer supported by buyer evidence, deliverable within capacity, and sustainable after owner effort and support?
Practitioner usefulness Chapter 1, the Practice Index, and one complete artifact chain. Can a reader produce the required record, recognize failure, repair it, and reuse it in the next task?

Evidence boundaries

TFIS supplies the primary philosophy and maturity model. Research supports particular claims within the scope stated in the chapters; it does not validate the entire blueprint. Audy and Himura Inc. are fictional, and their authored examples are teaching material. TFIS build-deck observations remain distinct from proposed next steps, verified operational performance, and commercial outcomes.

Treat a filled template as a record to inspect. Check whether it contains a real attempt, a manual rehearsal, an authored specimen, or an untested plan. Likewise, separate file integrity from truth, retrieval from correct use, a sound delivery from proven demand, and a sale from sustainable economics. These distinctions are essential to assessing the book’s execution claims.

The most useful review follows one dependency all the way through: question → evidence → instruction → bounded work → verdict → verified reuse → delivery decision. Identify where the chain holds, where the demonstration is missing, and the smallest relevant test that would resolve the gap.
