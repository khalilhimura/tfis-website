# Inquiry brief

## Worked sample: fictional Himura Inc. case

**Name:** `inquiry-brief-v1`.

**Parent:** The learner-task branch of `curiosity-map-v1`.

**Output:** Revise `workshop-brief-v1` while preserving uncertainties in `claim-evidence-ledger-v1`.

**Question:** What changes would make this synthetic action-list activity practice the stated learner task and expose unsupported additions?

**Situation:** Audy is designing a provisional thirty-minute workshop example. Its proposed adult learners would turn meeting notes into action lists. No workshop has been delivered and no customer need has been validated.

**Decision:** Whether the brief needs a corresponding practice input and checking criteria in addition to a clearer learner outcome.

**Starting answer:** Audy has provisional criteria from her own earlier inspection. Replacing “understand effective follow-up” with a specific action-list outcome may be enough to guide a fresh draft without carrying those criteria into its instructions.

**Alternative:** Better wording alone may leave the activity and review criteria unspecified.

**Evidence that would change the answer:** An agenda can use the revised outcome while never asking a learner to produce an action list; a polished list can pass a vague check while adding unsupported commitments.

### Synthetic input

1. The facilitator agreed to circulate the notes by Friday.
2. The group suggested asking the venue about access arrangements; nobody was assigned.
3. The group agreed to try a shorter introduction at the next session; no owner was assigned.

**Available evidence:** These invented notes, Audy's draft, and her comparison of input with output.

**Missing evidence:** Actual educator problems, learner attempts, delivery results, and willingness to pay.

**Requested output:** Revised practice instructions, a candidate action list, and a check against the notes.

**Criteria:** Preserve agreed actions; retain only stated owners and deadlines; separate suggestions from commitments; flag missing execution details; require a learner attempt before showing a completed answer.

**Missing-information rule:** Leave the gap visible and identify a useful clarification. Do not invent an owner, deadline, agreement, interview, or result.

**Cannot establish:** Learning gains, demand, or viability.

### Worked attempt and repair

**Faulty candidate:** “Facilitator: circulate notes and confirm access by Friday. Organizer: prepare a shorter introduction before the next session.”

**Review:** The first circulation commitment is supported. The access task was only suggested; its owner and deadline were not supplied. The introduction was agreed, but its owner was invented.

**Repair:** Keep the circulation commitment. Record the venue proposal as unresolved. Keep the agreed introduction change with the owner explicitly missing. Revise the practice instructions to require these distinctions.

**Fresh challenge:** “We could send a reminder on Thursday; a decision will be made tomorrow.”

**Expected review:** Thursday is a proposed timing, not a confirmed deadline. The reminder remains unresolved pending a decision.

**Unaided explanation:** An agreed action may still lack an owner. A possible action is not made into an agreement by putting it in a tidy list.

**Next question:** Can a different synthetic input expose a distinction the current criteria fail to cover?

## Acceptance criteria

- The brief can be understood without access to the earlier chat.
- The starting hypothesis was saved before the final answer.
- The question points to a decision and names a result that could change it.
- Each claim is supported by an appropriate input or is explicitly provisional.
- The output can be checked against criteria chosen before reviewing it.
- At least one observed defect leads to a targeted repair, or the record explains what meaningful challenge the output survived.
- A fresh example checks the important distinction rather than repeating the same successful output.
- You can explain the decision and identify a plausible error without consulting the assistant.

## Common failure and repair

**Failure:** The question asks for proof of business or learning outcomes using only a synthetic example.

**Repair:** Narrow the immediate decision to the design property the example can reveal. Name the actual external evidence needed for the larger claim.

**Failure:** The assistant returns a polished agenda when checking criteria were needed.

**Repair:** Specify the output directly and provide one acceptable example. Preserve the question if the question itself was clear.

**Failure:** The prompt asks why the preferred option is best.

**Repair:** Ask under what conditions each option would be useful, and request a concrete counterexample to the preferred choice.

**Failure:** You accept the output but cannot explain why.

**Repair:** Compare one correct and one faulty example, explain the difference, consult the evidence, and try a new pair.
