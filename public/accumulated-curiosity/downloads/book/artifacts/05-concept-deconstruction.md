# Concept-deconstruction record

Use with Chapter 5. This record makes a particular explanation and its assumptions inspectable; it is not a certification or a universal theory.

## Reusable template


## Worked example: Fieldwork

This is a fictional teaching example, including the review result.

**Decision:** Improve the feedback on the action-list exercise before adding another workshop activity. Retain Chapter 4's clear instructions, criteria, and individual attempt.

**Result:** A participant converts meeting notes into an action list that preserves commitments and identifies missing owners or dates.

**Observation:** The current synthetic brief specifies an action-list output and criteria. Its draft feedback says “incorrect” without explaining which source detail requires a revision.

**Constraint:** The illustrative session is thirty minutes. This is a design constraint, not an evidence-based universal duration.

**Choice:** Require a source statement for each listed action so the reviewer can inspect the distinction between an agreement and a suggestion.

**Assumption:** The examples make the distinction clear enough for a first attempt. This has not yet been checked with learners.

**Proposed operation:** Read one worked example → create an action list from different notes → explain each entry → compare with criteria → revise.

**Weak connection:** A correctness verdict does not yet tell the participant why an entry needs revision or how to apply the reason to another set of notes.

**Check:** Give a peer the notes, a faulty action list, and the feedback. Ask what to change and which source detail justifies the change.

**Illustrative result:** The peer recognizes the invented owner but interprets the feedback as a rule to remove every action that has no named owner.

**Revision:** Explain that an agreed action can remain with its owner marked unknown. Point to the sentence recording the agreement, then check the explanation with different synthetic notes.

**Limit:** This check can reveal whether the feedback communicates a usable reason to this reviewer. It does not establish general learning effectiveness, customer demand, or the appropriate workshop price.

## Prompt for reviewing your explanation

Replace the bracketed fields and include your own attempted explanation first.

```text
I am examining [CONCEPT OR PROCESS] to decide [SPECIFIC DECISION].

My current explanation:
[EXPLANATION]

Available observations and sources:
[EVIDENCE, INCLUDING LOCATIONS AND LIMITS]

1. Identify any missing operational steps or ambiguous terms.
2. Separate what I supplied from any inference you introduce.
3. Identify the assumption most likely to change this decision.
4. Propose one feasible check and explain what it could not establish.
5. Give a counterexample to the explanation if you can justify one.

Do not invent research citations, customer evidence, or trial results.
Mark factual claims requiring verification. Keep suggestions separate
from findings. I will review and choose the next action.
```

## Acceptance and repair

The record is usable when another reader can identify the result, follow the proposed operations, find an unresolved assumption, and connect a recorded observation to your revision. Keep the first version so the change is visible.

If the explanation consists mainly of broad terms, replace one term with an operation and its input/output. If the check cannot change the decision, choose a more consequential assumption. If you used simulated feedback, label it and arrange a real observation before relying on it as evidence about people.
