## Worked example: fictional Fieldwork

**Plan:** `solo-enterprise-plan-v1`.

The entire Fieldwork case is fictional. Its existing commercial events and all figures below are authored illustrations. The ninety-day actions are proposed future work, not completed events. No real customer, payment receipt, measured learner outcome, achieved annual revenue, company valuation, or advanced operational certification is supplied.

### Starting state

The [product experiment](21-product-experiment.md) contains three constructed discovery conversations, two proposals, and one agreed USD 300 pilot, PP21-01. Its PB21-v1 pack exceeded the thirty-minute brief; PB21-v2 repaired the agenda and was accepted for the educator's own rehearsal. No second sale is agreed. The [capability portfolio](23-capability-portfolio.md) preserves which demonstrations a real operator must still perform.

**Economic direction being investigated:** USD 1 million in gross annual revenue. Profit, owner income, collected cash, and valuation remain separate. This target is not a forecast or an outcome of the single pilot.

### Exact illustrative unit assumptions

| Assumption | Baseline pilot model | Proposed alternative |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unit price/revenue assumption | USD 300 | USD 750 |
| Unit cash cost | USD 20 | USD 35 |
| Delivery, including repair | 4 hours | 3 hours |
| Acquisition, including unsuccessful proposals | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| Support | 1 hour | 1 hour |
| Total owner time per unit | 7 hours | 6 hours |
| Owner-time value | USD 50/hour | USD 50/hour |

The baseline's USD 300 was the agreed fee; treating it as earned revenue is the explicit [unit-economics scenario](22-unit-economics.md). Collection is not observed. The USD 750 price, demand, cash cost, and six-hour efficiency are proposed assumptions, not verified improvements.

Baseline unit economic contribution: `300 − 20 − (7 × 50) = −70` USD.

### Monthly capacity and economics

Both scenarios assume 160 available owner hours per month, forty fixed operating hours, 120 remaining unit hours, and USD 200 fixed monthly cash cost. The owner-time allowance is not an additional cash payment in the cash-remainder row. As in Chapter 22, these monthly cash remainders assume all modeled revenue is collected and the listed cash costs are paid in the same period; no collection is observed in the pilot.

| Result under the stated assumptions | Baseline | Proposed alternative |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Whole-unit capacity | 17 | 20 |
| Unit hours used | 119 | 120 |
| Total owner hours, including fixed work | 159 | 160 |
| Revenue if all units sell and are earned | USD 5,100 | USD 15,000 |
| Unit cash costs | USD 340 | USD 700 |
| Fixed cash costs | USD 200 | USD 200 |
| Cash remainder before owner compensation and tax | USD 4,560 | USD 14,100 |
| Owner-time allowance | USD 7,950 | USD 8,000 |
| Economic contribution after that allowance | −USD 3,390 | USD 6,100 |

Neither row set supplies a buyer pipeline. The proposed month uses every modeled owner hour, with no additional contingency reserve. An actual commitment can be lower; record the chosen reserve and recalculate the result instead of counting reserved time as sold capacity.

### Annual target and the constraint it reveals

Twenty units × USD 750 × twelve identical fully sold months = USD 180,000 annualized revenue. Twelve such months are assumed, not forecast.

At USD 750, reaching at least USD 1 million in annual gross revenue requires 1,334 whole units, producing USD 1,000,500. Proposed annual capacity is only 240 units. At six owner hours each, the target would require 8,004 unit hours before fixed operations. The current design cannot supply that volume within the stated time allocation.

**Implication:** A different useful product or operating arrangement is needed to change the sales-to-owner-work relation. Additional agents do not create customers or eliminate human work merely because they can produce drafts concurrently.

### Constructed planning mistake and repair

**Mistake:** “The higher-price scenario has positive contribution, so this is now a million-dollar plan.”

**Repair:** Keep the positive modeled contribution conditional, then calculate annualized revenue and whole-unit capacity. Record the remaining demand, price, effort, and capacity questions. Do not label the proposed alternative validated or make commitments using a buyer count the record does not contain.

**Second mistake:** Counting the USD 14,100 cash remainder as disposable owner income while ignoring the owner-time requirement, obligations, collection timing, and tax exclusion.

**Repair:** Keep the exact metric label. Establish actual cash and obligations separately, and use the explicit owner-time allowance for the economic comparison rather than subtracting it twice as cash.

### Selected first route and alternatives

**Initial proposed route:** Continue investigating a bounded workshop-review and activity-redesign service. Examine a narrower reusable pack before promising a broader product or more volume. This is a proposed next experiment, not another agreed engagement.

**Educational alternative:** Test whether an educator can use a reusable pack with the described support. Purchaser acceptance and participant learning need separate evidence. Record support and maintenance rather than assuming digital delivery removes them.

**Software alternative:** Test a valuable interaction first, then automate a stable operation with inspectable source handling, authority, failure behavior, and recovery. Account for ongoing technical and support work. Do not build software solely to bypass unresolved demand.

**Current operating boundary:** `learning-loop-v1` still selects `instructions-v3`; no v4 is created here. The [runbook](20-enterprise-runbook.md), [review policy](18-review-policy.md), [verdict practice](13-verdict-ledger.md), and [portability drill](15-portability-drill.md) guide operation. Customer communication and consequential commitments remain with the human unless separately authorized. Earlier forty-five-minute internal simulation slots are not pilot delivery estimates.

### Constraints and review conditions

- Do not promise more work than the chosen capacity and reserve permit.
- Keep a source-dependent delivery pending if necessary evidence cannot be inspected.
- Revisit scope or price when actual acquisition, delivery, or support contradicts the model.
- Record whether a payment is agreed, earned under the stated treatment, invoiced, or collected; do not spend an assumed receipt in the model.
- Keep source-faithfulness and the learner's individual attempt/revision when redesigning the pack; a faster output with missing essential work is a different result.
- Reassess the route if the relevant buyers do not choose the defined offer. Interest is not a sale.
- Include the work and cost of any collaborator openly. Team choices follow the purpose and evidence, not the need to defend a label.
- Protect a stated allocation for maintenance, recovery, and exploration within the actual time budget.

## Acceptance criteria

- The primary economic metric, currency, period, and motivation are clear.
- Current evidence and future assumptions remain distinct.
- Price, units, cash cost, owner time, fixed work, demand, and whole-unit capacity are connected by correct arithmetic.
- Annualization is conditional, and a target is not reported as a forecast or outcome.
- The selected operating route states what must change and which new dependencies it creates.
- The ninety-day plan yields reviewable records and decisions, with conditions for stopping or changing course.
- Human judgment, authority, collaborator needs, and recovery remain explicit.
- The plan can be explained without AI and revised when its assumptions fail.

## Common failures and repairs

| Failure | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue, profit, cash, owner income, and valuation share one label. | Define the metric and preserve the costs, period, and collection state relevant to it. |
| A fully sold month is multiplied into a forecast. | Label the annualization and inspect demand, capacity, variation, and time off separately. |
| A higher price or lower delivery time becomes an accomplished improvement. | Keep it proposed until a relevant buying/delivery test supplies evidence. |
| Owner work disappears because an agent did the first draft. | Count acquisition, review, correction, support, and fixed operations in the appropriate categories. |
| Every modeled hour is promised without acknowledging variation. | Choose an operating reserve or lower commitment and recalculate the economics. |
| A new product form inherits the old cost assumptions. | Inspect its actual support, maintenance, delivery, and distribution work. |
| “Solo” hides outside labor or excludes needed expertise. | State the ownership model and include collaborators' roles, costs, and authority. |
| The plan allows only expansion. | Write evidence-based conditions for narrowing, choosing another route, or stopping. |
