Worked sample: fictional Fieldwork case

Project: Fieldwork; design practice, not customer research.

Current output: workshop-brief-v1, linked to claim-evidence-ledger-v1.

Parent attempt: The fifth practice specimen proposed an action-list task and provisional checking criteria. Its activity still needs to give each learner an actual opportunity to attempt that task.

Proposed benefit: Help an independent educator specify a useful learner task. This remains a business hypothesis; no demand has been observed.

Question Decision it could change What Maya observed in the fictional practice What remains unknown Evidence needed
Will the proposed activity produce an action list that someone can check against the commitments in the notes? The activity and its connection to the proposed task The fifth specimen names an action-list task and candidate criteria; the activity remains undecided Whether the activity requires an individual attempt and exposes the distinctions in the criteria Inspect synthetic notes, a candidate activity, and an action-list attempt
Do independent educators seek help defining learner tasks? Whether to pursue the service idea No customer evidence yet Actual preparation difficulties and willingness to seek help Future consent-based conversations about real preparation work
Would a digital form simplify preparation? Whether to add a tool No repeated form-related bottleneck established Whether a form would save effort at an acceptable standard Repeated manual preparation records, then a bounded comparison if warranted

Selected question: Will the proposed activity produce an action list that someone can check against the commitments in the notes?

Why now: The answer can improve the brief using material Maya can create and inspect herself.

Alternative postponed: A market inquiry matters, but synthetic design work cannot substitute for real educator evidence. Maya will prepare for that inquiry separately.

Starting expectation: Requiring an individual action-list attempt will make the relationship between the activity and the proposed outcome easier to inspect.

Input: Invented meeting notes containing a commitment, a suggestion, and an agreement with an unspecified owner.

Output: A proposed activity, a practice input, and a check that applies the existing candidate criteria to an actual action-list attempt.

Boundary: An illustrative forty-minute preparation session; no outside communication or real personal data.

Stopping condition: Maya can show whether the check detects an unsupported owner or deadline, or name the specific distinction she still needs to understand.

Counterexample: The revised outcome still permits an exercise with no learner action-list attempt.

Cannot establish: Learner improvement, educator demand, optimal workshop duration, or business viability.

Observed result in the fictional case: A discussion-only activity could cover the topic without producing an individual action list. A clean set of notes would also leave the distinction between suggestions and commitments untested.

Inspectable contrast: Discussion-only: “Discuss what makes follow-up useful.” Revised activity: “Individually turn the supplied notes into an action list, then compare the result with the notes and candidate criteria.” The inquiry-brief specimen supplies the ambiguous notes and faulty answer used to test this revision.

Revision: Carry the existing owner/date and commitment/suggestion criteria into the inquiry brief; use ambiguous notes and require an individual attempt before comparing answers.

Next inquiry: How should the brief connect the learner task, actual attempt, and acceptance criteria? Record it in inquiry-brief-v1.

Parked question: Could a digital form help assemble practice examples?

Return condition: Revisit only if repeated manual work identifies example assembly as a meaningful bottleneck.

Acceptance criteria

  • Another reader can locate the unfinished work and the parent attempt.
  • Every serious candidate question points to a decision, rather than only a topic.
  • Observations, assumptions, and missing evidence remain distinguishable.
  • The selected inquiry uses an evidence route appropriate to the claim.
  • The result changes the work or explains precisely why it cannot yet change.
  • A parked question has a return condition, and a next inquiry has a reason to exist.
  • Without the assistant, you can explain why you chose this question and what the attempt cannot prove.

Common failure and repair

Failure: The map becomes a long collection of interests, tools, and reading links.

Repair: Return to the unfinished output. Keep the questions that could alter its next revision. Move unrelated interests into open exploration without treating them as project obligations.

Failure: A synthetic example is recorded as proof of customer need.

Repair: Relabel it as design practice. Preserve the market question and specify the external evidence required to investigate it.

Failure: The session repeatedly runs past its boundary.

Repair: Reduce the promised output or choose a preparatory question. Record what remains unresolved instead of removing the stopping condition.
